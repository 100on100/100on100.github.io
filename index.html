Lossless raw imaging

Raw images kept exactly, in smaller files, with a decoder small enough to read.

On 67 raw camera sensors, 100on100 files are smaller than JPEG-LS and FFV1 and slightly larger than JPEG XL. The original version 4 C99 decoder library (small build) is 9,075 octets, and a region of a frame decodes on its own. Every damaged file in our test was decoded exactly or refused with a numbered reason.

Coder for single-channel and multi-plane images (format version 5)

On 67 raw camera sensors

On 67 raw camera sensors, as written files of the coder for single-channel and multi-plane images (format version 5), pooled size is 0.36% smaller than JPEG XL at effort 7 (36 of the 67 cameras smaller). JPEG XL effort 7 only: effort 9 was not measured on these files. JPEG XL file sizes are as recorded earlier, with 5 of the 67 re-encoded and equal.

Pooled means the sum of octets over the sum of samples, not per camera; the margin is thin, and 31 of the 67 cameras are larger than JPEG XL at effort 7, three of them well above (Nikon D7000 +27.2%). The table above is for versions 3 and 4 and stays true of them. This is one coding mode on one kind of data: JPEG XL at effort 9 produces smaller files than our version 5 colour coder (our files are larger by 10.5% on COCO-120, 8.6% on CC0 camera pictures in 8-bit sRGB, 1.6% in 16-bit linear, 26.4% on Wikimedia Commons-120). On hyperspectral data the format is behind CCSDS 123 (about 12 to 13%, stateless); on multispectral it is level (pooled). We do not publish speed figures.

Colour pictures · format version 5

The colour decoder, and where it stands

For colour pictures the format is locked and a reference decoder is built. This release includes a decoder for colour pictures.

Footprint

35,344 octets: the whole RISC-V decoder image, code and data (stack and heap reserved, not stored).

Density

Pooled bits per sample, version 5 colour coder: 3.5913 on 120 COCO pictures (8-bit), 3.5131 on 35 CC0 camera pictures (8-bit sRGB), 9.2552 on the same 35 pictures (16-bit linear), 2.6962 on 120 Wikimedia Commons pictures (8-bit); every file decoded exactly by the C library.

Among small decoders

Among coders whose decoder is at most 98,304 octets, the version 5 colour coder is the smallest in size on COCO-120, the CC0 cameras and Commons-120. Against JPEG-LS (CharLS, whole picture) its files are smaller by 21.0% / 6.2% / 9.9% on COCO / cameras 8-bit / cameras 16-bit and by 20.8% on Commons.

Where others are ahead: JPEG XL (effort 9) produces smaller files than the version 5 colour coder: by 9.5% on COCO-120, 7.9% on the CC0 cameras (8-bit sRGB), 1.6% (16-bit linear) and 20.9% on Commons-120. JPEG XL's decoder is 713,139 octets and does not meet the 98,304 cap. Commons-120 is a quality-filtered pool (selection bias). Decoder sizes are x86-64 builds, not RISC-V.

Hyperspectral, measured: On imaging-spectrometer scenes from NASA open data (EMIT, 5 scenes; AVIRIS-NG, orthocorrected, 3 scenes; 2 of 10 dropped for no-data fill), the version 5 coder's rate is larger than CCSDS 123's (untuned default settings): pooled by 13.7% (EMIT) and 8.1% (AVIRIS-NG) at one quantisation scale, and by 14.6% and 7.1% at a four-times finer scale; not within 5% on this data. Formula rates, not written files; one 512 x 256 window per scene; radiance scaled and rounded to 16-bit integers (not the sensors' native form); small scene counts; one very dark EMIT window is smaller at both scales. Data openly shared (LP DAAC, ORNL DAAC).

2 · Security and integrity

Damaged files are never passed off as good

In our test sets, every damaged or hostile file was either decoded exactly or refused with a numbered reason. The original decoder (format version 4) was tested against four standard lossless codecs, damaging the same files the same ways for each.

0 of 9,600

damaged files decoded wrong or went undetected. Every one was decoded exactly or refused by number

29.7 M

fuzzed inputs under AddressSanitizer and UBSan, with determinism and tile-consistency oracles: 0 issues

284 / 284

corpus files where the C99 decoder library equals the reference decoder octet for octet (97 of 97 test vectors too)

9,600

damaged files per codec, 48,000 in all: one bit flipped, 4 octets overwritten, 16 octets zeroed, cut short

Details on the evidence page. Test records are available to evaluators under NDA.

100on100 checks a checksum over its header and over every tile, so any change to the data is caught and refused. CCSDS 121 streams and JPEG 2000 codestreams carry no integrity check over the coded data, by design; JPEG XL and JPEG-LS catch most damage through their own decoding, but not all.

Method: libjxl 0.11.2, OpenJPEG 2.5.4, CharLS 2.4.3 and libaec 1.1.6, each built from its release source the same way (size-optimised, unused code removed, runtime libraries not counted). 24 raw camera crops (CC0) encoded losslessly by every codec; each file then damaged 400 ways (one bit flipped, 4 octets overwritten, 16 octets zeroed, cut short), identically for every codec: 9,600 damaged files per codec, 48,000 in all. Why now

Image decoders are being exploited in the wild

An image arrives from outside, and the decoder is the first code that touches it. Both of these are in the US government's catalogue of exploited vulnerabilities.

CVE-2023-4863 · WebP
“Google Chromium WebP contains a heap-based buffer overflow vulnerability that allows a remote attacker to perform an out-of-bounds memory write via a crafted HTML page. This vulnerability can affect applications that use the WebP Codec.”
CISA Known Exploited Vulnerabilities, added 2023-09-13. Google, 2023-09-11: “an exploit for CVE-2023-4863 exists in the wild.”
CVE-2025-43300 · raw camera files
“Apple iOS, iPadOS, and macOS contain an out-of-bounds write vulnerability in the Image I/O framework.”
CISA Known Exploited Vulnerabilities, added 2025-08-21. Apple: “may have been exploited in an extremely sophisticated attack.” Quarkslab traced it to lossless JPEG data inside DNG raw camera files, the same class of data 100on100 stores.

What the test covers

Our damaged-file test covers one bit flipped, 4 octets overwritten, 16 octets zeroed and truncation, on 24 camera crops. It does not cover larger images or every kind of damage, and we make no claim beyond the figures on this page.
